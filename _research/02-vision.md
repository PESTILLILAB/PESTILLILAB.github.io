Across more than 1,700 participants, from age 2 to 88, we found preferential connectivity between corresponding visual-field representations, and connectivity asymmetries aligned with perceptual asymmetries: perception is superior along the horizontal than the vertical meridian, and along the lower than the upper vertical meridian, and the connections between early visual areas show the same pattern. These results motivate projection rules specifying where information is preserved and where it is integrated as signals pass between areas. We are now establishing the axonal basis of these rules, testing whether adjacent early visual projections preserve retinotopic matching while higher-order projections distribute signals across broader representations, using macaque tracing and light-sheet imaging together with targeted human post-mortem imaging. These comparisons distinguish asymmetries arising from projection geometry from those introduced by tractography, and place anatomical constraints on models of perception and attention.
