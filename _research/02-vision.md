In more than 1,700 brains from age 2 to 88, regions of early visual cortex that represent the same part of the visual field are more strongly connected than regions that do not, and the connections are stronger along the horizontal than the vertical meridian and in the lower than the upper field, matching known differences in perception. From these data we are deriving projection rules: which pathways preserve retinotopic order and which mix it. We are now testing the rules at the axon level with macaque tracing, light-sheet imaging, and human post-mortem tissue, to separate real asymmetries from artifacts of tractography.
