Brain data can reveal mental states and, in principle, identify a person. As their value rises with AI, rules for moving data across borders are tightening, and the burden falls hardest on researchers in low- and middle-income countries. We mapped how 93 countries regulate neural data: transfer is almost always allowed in principle, always with conditions, and protection becomes unclear once data have been turned into features, embeddings, or trained models. We are building decision tools and modular agreements that let a collaboration act now rather than wait for harmonized law, and governance that software can read and enforce. The work brings together lawyers, ethicists, neuroscientists, regulators, and people with lived experience from Africa, Europe, and the Americas.
