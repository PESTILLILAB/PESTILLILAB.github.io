Tractography, the main way to map connections in living people, is indirect. Checked against tracer injections in animals, it gets the anatomy only partly right, and a model of the brain can fit the activity of every region and still assume the wrong route between them. So we measure the same brains at several scales, from single axons under the microscope to MRI, and use the comparison to calibrate human tractography and to build atlases that tie white-matter pathways to the brain's large-scale networks. The data run to petabytes, so the anatomy and the computing are developed together.
