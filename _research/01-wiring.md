Mapping human brain connections requires integrating measurements from micrometers to centimeters while preserving their anatomical relationships. Much of the detailed anatomical evidence informing connectivity models comes from macaques and rodents; in humans, diffusion tractography and functional connectivity provide valuable but indirect estimates, and leave unresolved whether an inferred connection corresponds to an actual axonal projection. A model can therefore reproduce regional responses while assuming an incorrect route of communication. We link micrometer-resolution measurements of axonal anatomy, from tract tracing and optical imaging in non-human primates, with MRI measurements that can be acquired routinely in the living brain, and we build the computational systems needed to integrate petabyte-scale anatomical data while preserving the relationships among tissue samples, spatial coordinates, reconstruction methods, and uncertainty. Our goal is predictive models of human brain connectivity that advance explanations of perception and cognition and, ultimately, inform surgical planning and predictions of disease progression and recovery.
